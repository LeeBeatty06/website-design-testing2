Zibaldone di Pensieri

The word zibaldone translates from Italian as “a heap of things” or a “miscellany”. This is a form of a commonplace book, but rather than indexing, it is more of a controlled chaos system.

Giacomo Leopardi

Zibaldone is associated with Giacomo Leopardi, who kept a massive diary called Zibaldone di pensieri. This book was kept between 1817 and 1832 and contained over 4,500 pages, containing reflections on philosophy, human development, memory, and society.

Although this was unpublished during his lifetime, it was released in the early 20th Century and is now recognized as a foundational text of modern culture.

Historical Context

The practice of keeping a zibaldone originated in the mid 14th century by literate citizens of cities like Florence and Venice in Italy. It was particularly helpful for merchants as they would use zibaldoni to mark information about different shipping, inventory, and taxes throughout the day.

These early papers would reflect things like tax records, currency exchange rates, medical notes, and then art and literature.

Unlike a commonplace book or other traditional diaries that prioritize indexing, a zibaldone was used for the sole purpose of collecting meaningful and personal text, regardless of its organization.
